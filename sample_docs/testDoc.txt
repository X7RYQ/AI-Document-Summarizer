AI and The end of Humanity [By Srivishva Melvin, sm25017]
This debate talks about AI in general and how it will affect humanity, for either the better or
the worse, and the speakers chosen for this topic were perfect as they are in the field already
and they are the best to speak about this soon-to-be issue that is raising rapidly.
The speakers are:
1) Eliezer Yudkowsky – He is a leading safety AI reseacher who has done plenty of speeches
about AI and has been against it due to fear of a catastrophe due to it.
2) Joscha Bach – A Cognitive scientist that has done researches to compare the similarities
and differences between the human mind and the one of an AI.
3) Liv Boeree – A former Poker Champion, philanthropist and is currently a leading figure in
the Effective Altruism Movement.
4) Scott Aaronson – One of the best/leading Quantum Complexity theorist in the world and
renowned for his research on quantum computing.
5) Gunes Taylor – The host of this debate and a molecular biologist who primarily researches
on reproductive glands of humans.
Each speakers are unique in the way they think about AI, as Eliezer feels more towards
getting afar from AI as it is still an unknown topic to us humans and experimenting on it
without proper research beforehand seems like a spell to cause destruction, or at the very
least a massive loss, of humanity; while Joscha thinks of AI like another species to live among
humans, that yes it may still be new but will learn through human interactions and perhaps
help in improving our current state of life we’re in. Liv and Scott on the other hand doesn’t
seem to have any strong points towards either and are more neutral, with some points feeling
more against AI while others may feel more towards the adoption of AI in our lives.
The main themes covered in this debate was if first AI could ever develop humans qualities
like emotions or even conciousness (which was the most critical topic that was spoken about
in my opinion), if there was any benefits to humans from investing their time and resources
into it and if we should continue developing it or fall back.
The way each and every speakers presented their views was beautiful to say the least.
They’ve described their views in a respectful yet strong manner, especially Eliezer and
Joscha, and even if at times I was to disagree with a few points, I still understood and in a
way, sympathizes as to why they have such an belief to a certain subject.
The main points of agreements was that AI is at its earliest and still needs rigorous
development to feel comfortable using it with full trust. As Eliezer and Scott pointed out and
Liv and Joscha agreed and improved on later, that AI stills remains an invention that has
changed humans for good. They have made us more dependent on their help, which in return
as reduced our attention span and also motivation to improve or get our work done on our
own. A point of disagreement was between Eliezer and the other speakers and that he saw AI
as a tool and nothing else but a tool while everyone else saw it as another entity that could
coexist with humans later on and I’ll have to side with Eliezer on this one because as it was
mentioned, “AI isn’t made out of flesh and hence cannot be deemed another entity” is a valid
point but the main difference between humans and AI that would remain a constant conflict
and difference is that humans can and probably will always have control over AI, or any
software of that sort, as its “conscience” is a line of code that can be read and modified by
qualified humans, but the same cannot be done vice versa, that is AI directly accessing the
human mind and changing it to how it pleases; which was what Eliezer was trying to get at
when saying that AI doesn’t have emotion but instead “predicts”. At the end of the day AI is
just a block of metal that has the entire internet’s knowledge inside and even then, makes
simple mistake and usually does not learn from them, as opposed to humans.
There was a point that was said by Eliezer and that I would to improve on, which was in brief,
that AI will know we exist alongside them but will not actually hurt or eliminate us just for the
sake of it. This point is an important one to keep in mind about handling AI because let’s say
in the near future, it does gain consciousness and knows its power, it wouldn’t go out of its
way to hurt a being inferior to it if it would gain noting, The same way us humans wouldn’t go
out of our way to hurt every passing ants, unless they bite us in which “they got in our way”
and hurting it would seem fair (I apologise for the rather gruesome example if that can be
called like that) and feeling that AI would think that way isn’t farfetched too, and if we all could
actually understand it then our fear about AI, like Gunes mentioned that she does speak
respectfully to AI out of “fear”, would be greatly reduced.
Another point where I do agree with is Joscha’s answer about the last theme (if humans will
be able to keep AI under control or not), in which he said that the world is divided into 2
groups, one who would think they can keep it under control, while the other feeling more like
retreating is the better option, and Scott went into details about why each groups think in that
way. Honestly, only time and further development will tell in which favor the final answer will
go to but I feel like for now, we’ll be continuing with the mindset of “AI can be kept under
control” but could switch at any moment if a misinput or an unusual output is shown from it.
The last I wanted to cover and the only point I disagree with is with Liv, who says that looking
at past history, the group of “higher” intelligence always won against the “lower” intelligence
and AI could do the same with humans, as she assumes that AI are the “higher” intelligence
group which I wholeheartedly disagree. We are the ones who built and created AI and even at
the state it is right now (having the entire internet’s knowledge inside it), it still makes silly
mistakes like the famous “2 + 2 = 5” incident where a user gaslighted chatGPT into believing
that 2 + 2 is 5 and not 4. This incident alone proved that even if on paper AI is smarter than
us, it is only “book-smart” and only predicts what we’ll do, if we were to be against it; while we
could use multiple methods other than manipulation to come out on top, and is the reason
why I say that humans are the “higher” intelligence and not AI.
The dress-code made the debate feel a bit more relaxed, which does reduce the importance
put into the topic from a spectator’s point of view, yet the voice and language used, apart from
Liv’s slip-ups at times, re-enforced the actual issue and “problem” AI may cause. But overall,
the environment of this debate clearly shows that we are listening to professionals who knows
the subject well enough to give their views and, perhaps, advices on it.
